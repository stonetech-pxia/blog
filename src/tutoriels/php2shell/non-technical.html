Before you start
  1. Make a backup of your website before you change anything.
  2. Never give your passwords to ChatGPT or any other AI, or to anyone who contacts you "to help".
  3. Don't run anything you don't understand. We will never ask you to download a file or a script. If a step feels risky, stop and ask a professional.

1Who looks after your website?

Do you get invoices from OVHcloud? If they say "Web Hosting", "Starter", "Personal", "Pro" or "Performance", choose "I pay a hosting company". If they say "VPS" or "Dedicated Server", choose "I have my own server".

2Send this message to whoever looks after your site

It's their job to fix this. Copy the message, fill in the square brackets and send it by email. Ask for a written reply.

Hello,

Two critical security flaws in PHP were fixed on 21 October 2026 ("PHP2Shell", CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, severity 9.2/10). They let an attacker take control of a server remotely.

For my website [your website address], could you please confirm in writing:
1. which PHP version it currently runs;
2. that it has been updated to a fixed version (8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27, 8.5.12 or later, or a distribution package that includes the fix);
3. if this hasn't been done yet, the date when it is planned;
4. that a full backup was made before the work.

Thank you,
[Your name]

Still no reply after 48 hours, even after a phone call? Then choose "I pay a hosting company" above (or "I have my own server" if your invoice mentions a VPS) and deal with it yourself.

2Make a backup

Most hosting companies already make an automatic backup every day. To make one yourself just before you act:

  • With WordPress: in the dashboard, go to Plugins → Add New Plugin, search for "UpdraftPlus", click Install Now and then Activate. Then go to Settings → UpdraftPlus Backups and click Backup Now.
  • Otherwise: in your hosting account, look for "Backup".
  • No Plugins menu in WordPress? Your account is not an administrator. Ask your hosting company for the backup, using the message further down.
  • Can't manage it? Ask your hosting company, using the message further down. They will do it for you.
Lost your logins? This often happens when someone else built the site.
  • Hosting account: click "Forgot password" and use the email address that receives the invoices.
  • Is the account in the name of the person who built the site? Contact your hosting company's support with an invoice you paid. They will tell you how to get access back.
  • Does your invoice only mention a domain name (such as .com or .org) or email addresses? Your website may be hosted somewhere else, for example by the person who built it. Go back to question 1 and choose "I don't know".

3Find your PHP version

PHP is the "engine" that runs your website. You need its full version number, with three numbers (for example 8.3.36).

Option A, if your site is built with WordPress (the easiest): in the dashboard, go to Tools → Site Health, open the Info tab, then open the Server section. The "PHP version" line shows the full number. No Tools menu? Your account is not an administrator. Go to option B.

Option B: in your hosting account, look for "PHP version", "PHP selector" or "PHP configuration". If it only shows two numbers (for example "8.3"), that's not enough. Go to option C.

Option C: ask your hosting company, using the message further down. They will reply with the exact number.

You don't need to download or install anything on your site for this. If someone offers you a "test file" or a "detection tool", say no.

4Read the result

Look at the first two numbers (the "series", for example 8.3), then at the third one:

You are protected if you have at least: 8.2.35 · 8.3.36 · 8.4.27 · 8.5.12. Example: 8.3.36 or 8.3.40 means you are protected. Nothing else to do.
Same series, but a smaller third number (for example 8.3.20): your hosting company needs to install the fix. Most of them do it on their own within a few days. Send them the message below to be sure. Does the number have extra letters (for example "8.3.6-0ubuntu")? The fix may already be there. Your hosting company will confirm.
Series 8.1, 8.0, 7.x or older: your site uses an abandoned version that will not get a fix. The solution is to move to a recent version (8.3 or 8.4). On a web hosting plan, this takes a few clicks in your hosting account ("PHP version", "PHP selector" or "PHP configuration").
  • Changing the version can break the site if its plugins are too old. Make the backup first. Then, if you can, update WordPress and its plugins (Dashboard → Updates).
  • If the site no longer appears after the change, switch back to the old version in your hosting account. Going back usually works straight away.
  • You can get step-by-step guidance with the prompt generator, or hand the job to a professional with the message below.

3Make a backup

In your provider's customer account, open your VPS and look for "Snapshot" (a complete "photo" of the server) or "Backup".

  • This option sometimes costs extra (a few euros or dollars a month, with an "Order" button). It's money well spent, but it may need to be approved.
  • No password for the customer account? Use "Forgot password" with the email address that receives the invoices.
  • Check that the snapshot shows today's date and time. If something goes wrong, the "Restore" button puts the server back in that state (any orders or bookings received in the meantime would be lost).

4Let an AI guide you, one command at a time

We do not provide any script. Never run a program you don't understand on your server, wherever it comes from.

Instead, the prompt generator below prepares a message that asks the AI to have you type short, standard commands, one at a time, that only read information (for example php -v, which shows the PHP version). Before each command, the AI must explain what it does. You paste the result back to it, and it tells you what to do next.

Your first connection to the server (SSH)

Your "SSH access" details are often in an old email that contains an IP address (numbers like 51.75.12.34), a username (for example debian) and a password.

  • In the terminal, you type the real address and the real password. You only hide the address in what you paste to the AI (replace it with MY-IP).
  • The first time, a question appears (Are you sure you want to continue connecting). Type yes.
  • When you type the password, nothing appears, not even asterisks. That's normal.
  • Only type the password when the line password: is shown. Never at any other time, and never in the AI chat.
  • To leave the server, type exit.
If the AI suggests a long command, one that downloads something (curl, wget), one that starts with rm, or one that contains sources.list, sed -i, full-upgrade, dist-upgrade or | bash, don't type it. Ask the AI to explain why it's needed.

Comfortable with a terminal? The developers page lists the diagnostic commands one by one, with a table to read the results.

What next?

  
    
    
  • Check that the PHP version is now fixed (WordPress: Tools → Site Health), and that your site works normally, including forms and the shop.
    • 
    
  • If your site was vulnerable after 17 October, ask your hosting company or contractor whether they have detected any suspicious activity.
    • 
    
  • Online shop or customer records: if personal data may have been stolen and you are in the EU or UK, you generally have to report it to your data protection authority
      within 72 hours (GDPR). Your hosting company or a professional can help you find out whether this applies.
    • 
    
  • Warning signs: strange pages or unfamiliar ads, redirects to another website, administrator accounts you don't recognize, a warning from Google or your hosting company.
      If you see any of these, contact your hosting company and call in a professional. Your national cybersecurity agency or a local web agency can also help.
